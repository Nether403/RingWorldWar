> **The ring should be the battlefield, the prize, and the resource being destroyed by the war.**

concept: The Last Rotation

### One-sentence pitch

**Two survival doctrines fight for control of a dying ring habitat, but every mech they construct and every missile they fire consumes or damages the world they are trying to save.**

Possible tagline:

> **Win the world before the war makes it uninhabitable.**

## What happened

The ring’s central solar filament is becoming unstable. Its shadow-square network, climate systems, and structural correction machinery have begun drifting out of synchronization.

The ancient **Spinal Network** can still execute one final planetary-scale operation, but there are two mutually exclusive choices:

### The Meridian Compact

A coalition of permanent arc-cities, industrial families, soldiers, and civil governments.

Their plan is to **Anchor the Ring**:

* Lock the current rotation.
* Stabilize the shadow panels over the most populated districts.
* Seal failing sections.
* Preserve the greatest number of people alive today.

The risk is that the repair may only buy a few generations. If the filament continues deteriorating, everyone remaining on the ring eventually dies.

### The Axiom Choir

A mobile civilization descended from maintenance guilds, sensor operators, and people integrated into the ring’s distributed control network.

Their plan is to enact **The Migration Protocol**:

* Dismantle selected ring sectors.
* Use their mass and the ring’s angular momentum to power enormous launch systems.
* Send a much smaller population, genetic archive, and machine civilization toward another star.

Their plan offers a future, but requires abandoning billions of people and destroying inhabited regions to build the evacuation system.

Neither side is simply evil. The Compact protects existing lives but gambles everyone’s future. The Choir guarantees some form of survival by deciding who and what will be sacrificed.

Both operations require control of the same Spinal Nodes. Activating either plan permanently prevents the other.

That is why negotiation has failed.

# The game hook

## “The artillery high ground is a direction”

On a conventional RTS map, artillery advantage comes from hills and range upgrades.

In *Ring World War*, it comes from **which side of the enemy you occupy**.

Your current simulation already makes antispinward fire substantially longer-ranged than spinward fire. Turn that from an unusual weapon property into the central territorial question of every match.

A player should constantly ask:

* Can I establish a battery on the enemy’s long-shot side?
* Can I force their Longbows to attack from the short direction?
* Should I march around the wrap rather than directly toward their base?
* Can I hide my relocation inside the approaching night band?

This gives you a game identity that can be explained in one sentence:

> **Surround the enemy around a circular world to attack them from the direction where physics favors you.**

# Ring-specific mechanics

## 1. Spinal Nodes should come in antipodal pairs

> **Launch-scope boundary:** Known standard-map pairs may be antipodal, but universal pairing is not canon. Only local pair control and pair-only Dominance are approved for LS-07; the strategic abilities listed below are not part of the slice.

At present, Spinal Nodes provide command capacity and Dominance. That works mechanically, but they still resemble conventional RTS control points.

Instead, place them in pairs directly opposite one another across the ring interior.

Holding one node gives ordinary Command capacity. Holding both members of a pair creates a **Spinal Alignment** and provides a major strategic ability:

* Enables accurate chord shots through that corridor.
* Reveals large enemy structures around the paired node.
* Generates Alignment toward the alternate victory condition.
* Allows a single mech to make a dangerous cross-ring chord insertion.
* Stabilizes structural damage between the two nodes.

The player must therefore connect territory on the ground with territory visibly hanging overhead.

A three-pair layout would be enough for the first map.

## 2. Make the moving night band a front line

The shadow squares already produce moving day and night, with solar generation, sensing, and missile visibility intended to react to darkness.

Make those effects dramatic enough to shape operations:

**Day**

* Strong solar generation.
* Longer ordinary vision.
* Units and construction are easy to observe from overhead.
* Energy defenses operate efficiently.

**Night**

* Solar networks weaken.
* Wisp cloaking engages faster.
* Radar becomes less reliable.
* Rocket exhaust is visible from much farther away.
* Compact units can use thermal searchlights but reveal themselves.
* Choir sensors preserve more of their normal range.

The approaching night band then becomes a moving timing window:

> “Their laser grid will lose power in 70 seconds. Launch when the shadow reaches them.”

That is much more distinctive than a generic day/night damage modifier.

## 3. Strategic objects should be visible in the sky

A ring has no conventional horizon. The enemy’s territory hangs above the player.

Do not hide everything behind standard fog of war. Use **two layers of intelligence**:

* Tactical units, defenses, and exact composition remain concealed.
* Bastions, silos, active Spinal Nodes, large construction projects, explosions, and rocket launches are visible overhead.

The player can therefore see that something important is being built without automatically knowing how to counter it.

For example:

* A new orange silhouette appears overhead.
* Its heat bloom suggests a Compact launch complex.
* You know where it is around the ring.
* You do not know whether it contains ballistic batteries, cruise missiles, or a chord silo.
* You must send a Wisp or use radar to identify it.

The enemy in the sky becomes information, not just scenery.

## 4. Give territory two fronts

Because the map wraps, a base should never have a safe rear edge.

You can reinforce this with a simple **Arc Link** system:

* Structures receive power and command connectivity through controlled Spinal Nodes.
* A base connected to its Bastion from both directions has redundant supply.
* Cutting one direction reduces energy efficiency.
* Cutting both isolates the arc and disables advanced production.

This makes wrapping matter economically. A small raiding force that travels around the “wrong” side of the ring can sever an entire defensive network.

You do not need a complicated truck-based logistics simulation. Connectivity between nodes is enough.

## 5. The war should physically damage the habitat

Introduce local **Structural Stress** for ring sectors.

Stress increases through:

* Heavy ballistic impacts.
* Chord-shot re-entry.
* Extracting material from structural salvage sites.
* Destroying Spinal infrastructure.
* Overloading ring-level abilities.

At increasing stress levels:

1. Sensors become noisy.
2. Construction slows.
3. Small quakes interrupt deployed Longbows.
4. Breach scars or impassable cracks appear.
5. The sector begins losing atmosphere and must be abandoned.

This creates a meaningful decision:

> Bombard the enemy’s fortress until it collapses, or capture it intact because you need that sector for your own survival plan?

I would keep stress **local**, rather than using an easily griefed global “everyone instantly loses” meter.

## 6. Expand chord shots into transportation

Your chord shot currently turns the ring interior into a missile shortcut.

Later, use the same concept for extremely limited transportation:

### Chord Coffin

A launcher fires one mech through the ring interior toward a friendly antipodal beacon.

* Everyone sees it crossing the sky.
* It cannot change course after launch.
* Defenders receive a landing-area warning.
* The mech arrives damaged or temporarily disabled.
* Losing the beacon may cause a catastrophic landing.

This is not a generic air transport. It is a high-commitment strategic insertion that only makes sense on a ring.

Directly piloting the mech during the landing and immediate assault would connect your RTS and takeover mechanics particularly well.

# Revised victory conditions

Keep Bastion destruction as the straightforward military victory, but add a thematic strategic victory.

## Alignment victory

> **Not approved for LS-07.** This remains a separate future proposal. Local Spinal Alignment does not end a match or execute either final protocol.

To enact its survival plan, a faction must:

1. Control both nodes in three Spinal pairs.
2. Maintain sufficient energy.
3. Keep local structural stress below the safety threshold.
4. Hold the Alignment for approximately 90 seconds.

During Alignment:

* The ring’s lighting visibly changes.
* The solar filament intensifies.
* Every player can see the active nodes overhead.
* Chord weapons become more accurate.
* The defending faction receives emergency production or reduced build times.
* The attacker must defend several separated locations rather than one final superweapon.

This is a more ring-specific version of your existing Dominance system, rather than a completely new subsystem.

# How the existing factions fit

Your implemented modifiers already point toward these identities:

* Compact mechs have more health and their ballistic weapons are cheaper.
* Choir construction is faster and its vision is stronger, at the cost of mech durability.

Lean into that.

| Faction              | Strategic identity                                    | Battlefield style                                                |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Meridian Compact** | Hold fixed territory and preserve existing cities     | Durable mechs, fortified artillery arcs, local structural repair |
| **Axiom Choir**      | Move with changing conditions and control information | Rapid deployment, superior sensors, interception, relocation     |

Their current mech abilities—shield wall, siege mode, cloak, and interception umbrella—already support a game about protected advances, reconnaissance, and directional artillery warfare.

Avoid making every unit faction-exclusive immediately. A more achievable first step is to give shared units different doctrine bonuses and one faction-specific ring interaction.

For example:

* **Compact: Anchor Protocol** — temporarily reduces local structural stress and maintains solar output as night arrives.
* **Choir: Phase Protocol** — accelerates construction and preserves sensor coverage inside a moving shadow band.

# A possible match arc

### Opening: Read the ring

The player scouts salvage, observes the direction of the shadow front, and chooses which member of the nearest Spinal pair to contest.

The shortest path to the enemy is not automatically the correct path. The player may expand the long way around to obtain the favorable artillery direction.

### Midgame: Fight for direction

Longbows and rocket batteries appear.

Players fire, become revealed, relocate, and attempt to establish a launch position on the enemy’s antispinward side. Night bands create temporary weak points in power and sensing.

Capturing the second half of a Spinal pair unlocks the first chord corridor.

### Endgame: Align or destroy

One faction begins Alignment across three paired nodes.

The opponent must attack several locations around the ring, launch a chord strike against an overhead node, or ignore the network and attempt to destroy the enemy Bastion.

Meanwhile, heavy warfare is destabilizing the most contested sectors. Previously valuable ground may become dangerous or unusable.

# What I would prototype first

Do not begin with destructible terrain, mobile bases, or multiple campaigns. The smallest version that can prove the concept is:

1. Arrange six Spinal Nodes into three visible antipodal pairs.
2. Replace passive Dominance with Alignment generated only by completed pairs.
3. Make day/night strongly affect solar power, sensing, cloaking, and interception.
4. Add a clear map overlay showing the favorable artillery direction around any selected target.
5. Make silos, Bastions, node activation, and launches visible on the overhead ring despite tactical fog.

Those changes primarily reuse systems you already possess: Spinal capture, Dominance scoring, shadow-square lighting, energy, visibility, the unrolled minimap, trajectory simulation, and chord weapons. They would make the ring central without requiring another year of content production.

The revised core loop would become:

> **SCOUT THE LIGHT → CLAIM A SPINAL PAIR → CIRCLE FOR BALLISTIC ADVANTAGE → STRIKE AND RELOCATE → ALIGN THE RING BEFORE THE WAR BREAKS IT**

That feels like *Ring World War*, rather than an ordinary RTS placed on a spectacular curved map.
