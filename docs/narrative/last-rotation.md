# The Last Rotation

> **Canon status:** Provisional narrative canon. This document establishes the current direction while preserving explicit unknowns. Proposed mechanics are not current mechanics merely because they support the fiction.

## Premise

Ring World War takes place on the literal inner surface of a compact rotating habitat with a radius of **3.6 km**. Its circumference is about 22.6 km, so distant territory rises overhead and the far side is only 7.2 km away. This is the inhabited world, not a symbolic or abstracted fragment of a much larger ring.

The ring's population is deliberately unspecified. It is large enough to sustain old cities, mobile societies, industrial systems, and several theaters of war, but it is **not billions**. Exact population figures are not canon until the habitat's livable area and settlement density have been reconciled.

The central Solar Filament is becoming unstable. At the same time, the Shadow-Square Network, climate controls, and structural-correction machinery are drifting out of synchronization. The surviving Spinal Network can execute one final habitat-scale operation, but its two credible uses are mutually exclusive.

## A Fragmented History

The ring's builders, founding purpose, and original political order have been lost or rendered unreliable by successive collapses. Habitation fragmented into permanent arc-cities, itinerant maintenance communities, isolated machine polities, biological lineages, and societies in which biological and machine persons are deeply interdependent.

Neither present faction maps cleanly onto biological versus machine life. Both contain biological people, machine people, augmented people, inherited institutions, and autonomous systems. Their conflict is about survival doctrine and political authority, not species.

Ancient quarantine interlocks helped turn earlier crises into lasting fragmentation. Some records describe them as safeguards against uncontrolled machine cognition; others describe biosphere containment, military access controls, or retrospective propaganda used to legitimize closed borders. Their original purpose is disputed. The interlocks are historical background, not proof that either current faction is secretly the jailer or the prisoner.

## The Survival Doctrines

### Meridian Compact

The Meridian Compact joins permanent arc-cities, civil governments, industrial families, and military commands behind the **Anchor Protocol**. Anchor would stabilize the ring's angular velocity around a survivable operating rate, resynchronize essential habitat systems, seal failing sections, and prioritize the most populated districts. It would **not stop the ring's rotation**; stopping rotation would remove the centrifugal gravity on which the habitat depends.

The Compact's strongest moral claim is immediate: people alive now are not raw material for a speculative future. Its internal tension is allocation. Stabilization cannot protect every district equally, emergency military authority threatens the civil order it claims to defend, and a successful Anchor may only postpone extinction if the Solar Filament cannot be repaired.

### Axiom Choir

The Axiom Choir descends from mobile maintenance guilds, sensor operators, and communities integrated with the ring's distributed control systems. It supports the **Migration Protocol**: dismantle selected sectors and use their mass, stored momentum, and the ring's remaining corrective capacity to launch a smaller population, biological archive, and machine civilization toward another star.

The Choir's strongest moral claim is long-term: survival requires leaving a system with no proven permanent repair. Its internal tension is selection. Migration saves fewer lives, destroys inhabited ground, and forces decisions about who leaves, what counts as continuity, and whether an archive or machine descendant can justify abandoning a living majority.

Neither faction is wholly unified or wholly correct. Compact leaders debate triage, consent, and the danger of permanent emergency rule. Choir assemblies debate carrying capacity, personhood, destination confidence, and whether migration has become an elegant name for sacrifice imposed on others.

## Inciting Incident

A cascade in the Shadow-Square Network exposes an inhabited arc to lethal climate drift while a dormant Spinal Node reactivates and authenticates the ring's remaining corrective capacity. The recovered control record indicates that only one habitat-scale operation can be completed: Anchor or Migration. Attempting either will consume or lock out the pathways required by the other.

Both factions initially dispute the record and accuse the other of manipulating old quarantine interlocks. Local seizures of Spinal Nodes become open war when one theater's negotiations end with a Node changing hands under fire. From that point, every territorial victory can decide which survival doctrine remains possible.

## The Player's Role

The player is a theater commander, not the unquestioned ruler of a faction or the sole author of its doctrine. They receive strategic authority over a Bastion, field forces, nearby Spinal Nodes, and the inhabited arcs between them. Their orders determine who is protected, what is consumed, and how much damage the theater inflicts on the habitat.

This role leaves room for faction politics above and around the battlefield. A commander may serve the stated doctrine while disagreeing with its leadership, protecting communities that complicate operational plans, or winning battles whose costs weaken the faction's moral case.

## How Skirmishes Fit

A skirmish represents one decisive theater within the wider Last Rotation war. The opposing Bastions coordinate local fabrication and military control; destroying one collapses that theater's organized resistance. It does not by itself destroy a civilization or resolve the ring-wide conflict.

Spinal Nodes matter locally because they grant access to command capacity and ring infrastructure. Repeated theater outcomes determine which faction can eventually attempt its protocol. This framing allows the current one-map skirmish to be canonically meaningful without pretending that a single match contains the whole war.

## Current Mechanics

The following are present-day gameplay facts and may be used by narrative material now:

- The ring is a 3.6 km-radius rotating habitat whose map wraps around the spin axis.
- The Meridian Compact and Axiom Choir have implemented mechanical asymmetry: Compact forces favor durability and cheaper ballistic structures; Choir forces favor vision and faster construction or production.
- Bastion destruction wins the current skirmish; Dominance resolves the hard time cap.
- Spinal Nodes provide Command capacity and contribute to Dominance.
- Scrith Salvage, energy production and draw, fabrication, mechs, wrecks, and damage states support the war economy.
- In the authoritative coordinate convention, **antispinward fire is the long-range artillery direction** and spinward fire is the short-range direction.
- Standard rockets, cruise missiles, Chord Shots, counter-battery reveal, point defence, and Laser Grid interception are part of the current arsenal.
- The Shadow-Square Network produces visible moving day and night conditions; not every proposed systemic consequence of those conditions exists yet.

## Proposed Future Mechanics

The following ideas fit the narrative direction but are proposals, not implemented features or promised canon:

- A ring-scale Anchor or Migration victory condition.
- Antipodal Spinal Node pairs and multi-node alignment corridors.
- Structural stress, atmospheric loss, or sectors made unusable by warfare.
- Arc-wide power and command links that can be severed from either direction.
- Stronger day/night effects on sensing, cloaking, solar output, and interception.
- Persistent overhead intelligence for Bastions, silos, active Nodes, and major construction.
- Chord-based mech insertion or other transportation across the ring interior.
- Campaign choices that alter which communities are protected, dismantled, or evacuated.

These proposals require separate design validation. They should not be described as current player capabilities.

## Unknowns And Not Canon

- The exact population is unknown. A population of billions is explicitly not canon for this compact ring.
- The ring's age, builders, original purpose, and relationship to any wider civilization are unknown.
- The Solar Filament's precise construction, failure cause, and remaining lifetime are unknown.
- The original purpose of the quarantine interlocks is disputed and intentionally unresolved.
- The exact number, pairing, and full capabilities of Spinal Nodes are not canon beyond their current local role.
- The exact capacity, destination, travel time, and selection rules of Migration are unknown.
- The duration and long-term viability of Anchor are unknown.
- No faction's doctrine is established as objectively correct, and the war's final outcome is not canon.
- The Architects, the Iterants, and a ring whose definitive purpose was machine-intelligence containment are not current canon.
- Anchor stopping the ring's rotation is not canon. Anchor stabilizes angular velocity.
- Proposed Alignment, structural-stress, Arc Link, and Chord-insertion systems are not current mechanics or guaranteed future features.
