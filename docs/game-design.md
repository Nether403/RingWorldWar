# Ring World War — Game Design Document

## 1. High Concept
**"Command & Conquer meets Kerbal ballistics on a Halo ring."**
A real-time strategy game on the inner surface of a rotating ring megastructure. Because the world curves up and wraps around, war is fought in three registers: ground maneuver (mechs), over-the-horizon bombardment (rockets), and the psychological register of *seeing the enemy's territory hanging in your sky*.

## 2. The World
- **The Ring:** canonical gameplay ring: radius **3.6 km**, surface width **4 km**, rotating to produce **6 m/s²** of centrifugal gravity. The far side is 7.2 km overhead and the circumference is 22.6 km, making the overhead enemy and wrap-around flank readable during a match.
- **Topology:** the map wraps in the ring (spinward) direction — a cylinder. The rim walls bound the map in the axial direction. No corners to turtle in; every base can be flanked from both spinward and antispinward.
- **Shadow squares:** an inner ring of panels casts moving day/night bands across the surface. Night matters: solar power drops, sensor ranges shrink, rocket launches are easier to spot (visible plumes).
- **Terrain:** ruined megastructure districts, scrith plains, breach scars (holes to space, impassable), overgrown arcologies. Terrain provides cover, chokepoints, and salvage.

## 3. Factions (2, mechanically asymmetric)
- **The Meridian Compact** — a coalition of permanent arc-cities and civil, industrial, and military institutions. Visual identity: amber/orange, angular plating, banners, kinetic brutality. Implemented doctrine: tougher mechs and cheaper ballistic structures.
- **The Axiom Choir** — a mobile society descended from maintenance, sensor, and machine-integrated communities. Visual identity: cyan/white, smooth composite shells, holographics, drone swarms. Implemented doctrine: stronger vision and faster construction or production.

Both factions include biological and machine people. Further roster, silhouette, and ring-system differentiation remains future work.

## 4. Core Loop
```
SCOUT → EXTRACT → BUILD → POSITION → STRIKE → BREACH → (expand, repeat) → DESTROY BASTION
```
Minute-to-minute: queue economy, place structures, rally units, watch threat map.
Every ~3–5 minutes: a strategic beat — a rocket volley, a mech push, a territory grab.
Match arc (20–40 min): scramble for expansions → artillery duels and interception chess → mech spearhead endgame.

### Session flow
1. **Opening (0–8 min):** one Bastion (HQ), a handful of engineers. Scout terrain, claim extractors, establish power.
2. **Midgame (8–25 min):** contest salvage fields; unlock rocket batteries and interception; first mechs walk off the assembly line; over-the-horizon skirmishes begin.
3. **Endgame (25–40 min):** strategic rockets threaten bases; mech lances breach defense lines; destroy the enemy Bastion to win.

## 5. Resources & Economy
Three resources; two harvested, one generated:
- **Scrith Salvage (S):** mined from ruin fields by extractor structures + hauler drones. Primary build currency. Finite per deposit → forces expansion and map contests.
- **Energy (E):** generated by solar arrays (day-dependent, cheap) and fusion cores (constant, expensive). Consumed as *upkeep*: radar, shields, interception lasers, and rocket guidance all draw power. Firing during a blackout = dumb rockets.
- **Command Points (C):** cap on concurrent mechs (not mass). Earned by capturing **Spinal Nodes** — neutral megastructure control towers spread around the map. Ties the mech power fantasy to map control, not just economy snowball.

Design intent: S drives *quantity*, E drives *quality/activation*, C drives *map aggression*.

## 6. Rocket Warfare (pillar #1)
Rockets are the strategic layer. All rocket flight is truly simulated in the rotating ring frame — no faked hit-scan.

### 6.1 Trajectory classes
- **Ballistic arc (surface-to-surface):** the workhorse. In the implemented convention, **antispinward shots travel much farther** because they subtract from inertial floor speed; spinward shots are shorter. The live trajectory ribbon makes this asymmetry legible before firing.
- **Chord shot (endgame):** heavy missiles that leave the atmosphere and cut across the ring's interior, hitting the far side in a fraction of surface travel time. Devastating, expensive, and visible to everyone — a glowing lance crossing the sky. Counterable only by dedicated interceptors.
- **Cruise (low & slow):** terrain-hugging, immune to long-range interception, blockable by point defense and terrain. Good vs radar-heavy turtles.

### 6.2 The targeting/intel game
- **Fog of war** on the wrapped surface. Long-range fire needs a *spotter*: radar structure, scout mech, or recon drone with line of sight.
- Firing reveals the launcher's position for several seconds ("counter-battery flash"). Artillery duels become poker: fire → relocate (mobile launchers) or fortify (static silos hit harder but can't run).
- Night bands: launches are visually spectacular and visible to all — informational tradeoff.

### 6.3 Interception
- **Point-defense turrets:** shred cruise missiles and slow ballistics in terminal phase; consume Energy per shot.
- **Midcourse laser grid:** expensive structures that burn ballistic rockets at apex — but only inside their coverage arc, creating map geometry to play around.
- **Saturation doctrine:** interception has cooldowns/energy costs, so massed cheap rockets can overwhelm quality defense. Quantity is a quality.

### 6.4 Player UX
- Firing UI shows a live predicted trajectory ribbon (with Coriolis bend) and impact ellipse before committing.
- Impact zones telegraph on the ground for defenders with radar coverage — dodgeable by mechs, not by buildings.

## 7. Mech Combat (pillar #2)
Mechs are the tactical layer and the game's muscle fantasy. Few, expensive, persistent — closer to heroes than to blobs.

### 7.1 Classes (vertical slice: 4)
- **Vanguard (brawler):** shield wall, short-range autocannons, can body-block chokepoints. The anchor.
- **Longbow (artillery walker):** deployable siege mode — becomes a mobile rocket battery using the full ballistic sim. Bridges the two pillars: your rocket game walks.
- **Wisp (scout):** fast, cloaked while stationary, best spotter in the game. Enables deep-fire missions.
- **Aegis (anti-air/anti-missile):** mobile point defense umbrella. Escorts pushes through rocket alley.

### 7.2 Control model
- **Tactical:** classic RTS orders — move, attack-move, focus fire, stances, ability hotkeys.
- **Direct control (takeover):** hit a key on any selected mech → camera drops to third-person over-the-shoulder; WASD + mouse torso aim, abilities on keys, exit back to tactical any time. While piloted, the mech gets modest accuracy/reaction bonuses — piloting is powerful but you stop commanding the map. That tension is the design.
- Support squads (light vehicles, drones) exist as cheap swarm units so mechs feel huge by contrast, but mechs are the stars.

### 7.3 Feel requirements (non-negotiable)
- Weight: footstep camera thumps, ground decals, IK feet on terrain, slow torso traverse on heavies.
- Damage states: armor plates shear off, exposed internals glow, limping at low HP, spectacular reactor-death explosion.
- Every mech kill should feel like an event, not attrition.

## 8. Structures (vertical slice roster)
- **Bastion** — HQ; build radius origin; losing it loses the match.
- **Extractor** (S) · **Solar Array / Fusion Core** (E) · **Spinal Uplink** (captures nodes → C)
- **Fabricator** — support units + haulers · **Mech Foundry** — mechs
- **Rocket Battery** (ballistic) · **Silo** (chord, endgame) · **Point Defense** · **Laser Grid** · **Radar Mast** · **Wall/Gate**

## 9. Victory, Loss & Match Pacing
- **Win:** destroy the enemy Bastion.
- **Anti-stall:** Spinal Nodes trickle a "Dominance" score; at a hard time cap (45 min), higher Dominance wins. Turtling forever is not viable — the map decides.
- **Comeback lever:** losing your army but not your Bastion leaves you rocket options; a well-read chord volley can decapitate an overextended winner.

## 10. AI Opponent (single-player)
Two layers, mirroring the architecture:
- **Strategist:** utility-scoring goal picker (expand / tech / harass / all-in) reading economy + map control; cheats not on resources but on APM ceiling per difficulty.
- **Tactician:** squad-level behavior trees — kiting, focus fire, retreat thresholds, artillery reposition after firing, dodge telegraphed impact zones.
Difficulty tiers adjust reaction time, intel usage, and doctrine variety — never free resources on Normal.

## 11. Controls (desktop-first)
- RTS standard: edge/MMB pan, wheel zoom (zoom out far enough and the camera tilts to reveal the ring arc — the "strategy postcard" shot), ctrl-groups, tab subselect.
- `V` — takeover selected mech · `Esc` — back to tactical · `Space` — jump to last alert.

## 12. Out of Scope (current skirmish)
Multiplayer netcode, campaign/story missions, >1 map, faction-exclusive rosters, replays, mod support, and mobile/touch. All are architected-for where applicable, but not built.
