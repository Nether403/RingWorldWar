# Procedural Asset Generation

**Gameplay-rendering constraint:** terrain, 3D units, structures, effects, and
world-space presentation remain generated in code. Human-reviewed generated
cinematic media, sampled voices, and optional DOM dossier/transmission images may
ship through typed manifests with provenance and complete fallbacks.

**Why this can still look expensive:** the things that read as "AAA" are lighting, atmosphere, coherent materials, silhouette design, animation weight, and post-processing — none of which require downloaded assets. What procedural generation costs us is *hand-authored uniqueness*; what it buys us is perfect stylistic coherence (impossible to have mismatched assets when one system makes them all), a sub-megabyte download, and infinite variation for free.

The style therefore leans **hard-surface industrial sci-fi**: machined panels, greebles, trim, wear streaks, emissive strips. This is the one art style that procedural generation is genuinely excellent at, and it happens to be exactly right for mechs on a megastructure.

## 1. Meshes

### Mechs & structures — the greeble compiler
A small parametric modeling DSL that builds hard-surface shapes from primitives:
- **Chassis blocks:** boxes with per-face bevels and chamfers (generated, not modifiers).
- **Panel-lining:** recursive subdivision of a face into panels with inset gaps — the single highest-value technique for making primitives read as machinery.
- **Greebles:** seeded scatter of small boxes/cylinders/pipes over faces, density-mapped so they cluster at joints and vents.
- **Trim & piping:** swept tubes along edge paths, hydraulic cylinders at joints.
- **Emissive strips:** inset channels tagged with the faction emissive material.

Each mech is a `MechDef` — a tree of parts (torso, hip, upper/lower leg, foot, shoulder, arm, weapon) with proportions, bevel amounts, greeble seeds, and socket transforms. Change the seed → a new but stylistically-identical mech. Change the proportions → a new weight class. This is how we get roster breadth from one system.

Everything is welded into an indexed `BufferGeometry` per part, merged per LOD, and drawn instanced. LODs are generated by dropping greeble tiers (LOD1 = no greebles, LOD2 = chassis blocks only, LOD3 = a single billboarded silhouette).

### Terrain
Multi-octave domain-warped simplex noise + erosion approximation (a cheap thermal/hydraulic pass baked once at map generation), carved by hand-placed splines for canyons and breach scars. Wrapped seamlessly in the ring direction by sampling noise on a cylinder (3D noise on a circle) rather than tiling a 2D plane — this makes the seam mathematically impossible.

### Ruins & props
An L-system/grammar building generator: footprint → extrude → floors → facade grammar (windows, buttresses, pipes) → damage pass (boolean-ish chunk removal, rubble scatter at the base). A dozen grammar seeds produce an entire ruined city.

## 2. Materials & Textures
All maps generated into `DataTexture`s at load time on a worker, or computed directly in the shader where cheaper.

- **Base library:** scrith (dark composite), painted metal, raw steel, dust, rubble, glass, emissive.
- **Generation:** layered noise (FBM, Worley, ridged) → albedo/roughness/metalness/normal. Normals derived from the height field by finite differences.
- **Wear:** curvature-driven edge wear (computed from geometry, applied via vertex color) and downward-streaking grime — the two effects that most reliably make CG surfaces stop looking clean and fake.
- **Panel detail:** procedural panel-line normal/AO pattern applied triplanar, so even a bare box reads as a machined surface.
- **Faction color:** a mask channel recolored per instance — one material serves both factions.
- Textures are generated once into a small texture array and shared; no per-unit textures exist.

## 3. Sky & Atmosphere
Entirely shader-based, which is where procedural generation wins outright:
- Analytic atmospheric scattering (Preetham/Hosek-style) for the sky dome.
- **The ring arc is real geometry**, not a skybox — the far side of the ring is drawn as the far-tier shell, lit by the same sun, with aerial perspective applied. That's why it will look correct from every angle.
- Shadow squares: procedurally placed occluder bands that both darken the sky band and drive the sun's intensity/direction over the day cycle.
- Stars: a generated point cloud with realistic magnitude and color-temperature distribution.
- Volumetric shafts: raymarched against the shadow-square occlusion function.

## 4. Animation
No authored keyframes. Mech locomotion is **fully procedural**:
- Gait driven by a phase clock; feet placed by raycast against terrain, with IK (two-bone analytic solver) resolving knee/hip.
- Body bob, roll, and pitch derived from foot contact state and acceleration.
- Torso aims independently at the target; arms follow with a lag spring.
- Recoil, hydraulic settle, and damage limp are additive layers on top.

Procedural locomotion is *better* than authored animation here: it adapts to arbitrary terrain slope and speed automatically, which is exactly what a curved wrapped world demands. It's also the main source of "weight" in the game feel.

## 5. VFX
GPU particle system with procedurally-generated sprite atlases (smoke puffs, sparks, fireballs drawn into a canvas with layered noise at init). Contrails are generated ribbon geometry. Explosions are a composition of: expanding shockwave shader ring, particle fireball, real point light flash, debris instances, and a persistent smoke column.

## 6. Audio
WebAudio synthesis graphs, no samples:
- **Mech footfalls:** filtered noise burst + low sine thump + servo whine (FM).
- **Weapons:** noise + pitch-swept oscillator through a shaped envelope and distortion.
- **Explosions:** layered noise bursts with cascading low-pass sweeps and convolution-free reverb approximation via multi-tap delay.
- **Music:** generative ambient — evolving pad chords from detuned oscillator stacks, a slow harmonic progression driven by match tension, plus percussion that enters during combat.
- Distance is modelled with delay (sound arrives late) + low-pass (air absorption) + reverb send, which sells scale for free.

## 7. Fonts & UI
System font stack (no webfont downloads). UI is DOM/CSS with procedurally generated SVG icons — icons are drawn from the same greeble/panel vocabulary as the models so the interface and the world share a visual language.

## 8. Determinism
All generators take an explicit seed. The same seed always produces the same world, mech, and material — required for the determinism contract and for reproducible bug reports.

### Presentation/simulation boundary

Procedural presentation must not consume simulation RNG, mutate simulation
state, or affect stable entity ordering. Visual and audio seeds derive from
immutable namespaced inputs such as world seed, asset kind, and stable entity ID.

Phase 3A ruins and debris are dressing only. They have no collision, navigation
cost, line-of-sight effect, cover value, salvage value, damage, or targeting
identity. A gameplay-bearing ruin would require separate deterministic
simulation data and a new balance decision.

Gameplay source assets are TypeScript, GLSL, CSS, generated in-memory geometry,
generated `DataTexture`s, SVG, and WebAudio graphs. Authored `.blend`, `.glb`,
image textures, binary LUTs, and committed screenshots remain forbidden while
the procedural-only gameplay rule is active. Reviewed sampled speech and DOM-only
presentation images are the bounded exception; they cannot carry gameplay state.

## 9. Budget
Total download target: **< 1 MB** (JS bundle only). Generation cost at load: **< 3 s** on a mid-range machine, run on workers with a progress bar, and cached in IndexedDB where the result is deterministic and expensive.
